#permanent/hub  

A Grand Slam Offer as defined by  is an offer that makes you a lot of profit. 

### What is an offer
- Agreement on: 
	- What you give to the customers 
	- What the customers have to give you
	- How the transaction will happen (both ways)

### What makes a grand slam offer
The key to making a grand slam offer is "differentiation," and charging a premium for it. 
- Be in the right market
	- [[The key to business success is to have starving customers]]
	- [[Picking the right market is the number-one key to business success]]
		- Lots of Pain
		- Purchasing Power
		- Easy to target (Accessible)
		- Growing
	- [[You should niche down your product to solve specific problems so that you can charge more]]
- Premium & Differentiated pricing
	- [[Commodity VS Differentiated Pricing]]
	- **Differentiated pricing allows you to have much more profit**
	- [[Premium price is good for a business]]
- Exceptional and Differentiated value proposition
	- [[Delivering value in business is about increasing what customers want, and decreasing what they don't want]]
	- **To create a Grand Slam Offer:** 
		- Define customers dream
		- Identify problems
		- Ideate solutions
	- You need to create solutions for ALL problems customers can potentially have, otherwise they might not buy 
	- You should get demand going first before standardizing stuff
	- A good offer should solve ALL problems related to customers' aspiration
	- **Refining Solutions**
		- From the list of ideas, try to find ways to trim down the solution as much as possible so that you can scale
- Exceptional presentation of the offer 
	- Use Scarcity: Make them feel that the availability is limited
		- [[People want something that they can't have]]
		- Super Premium Price = Super Scarce x Super Value
		- [[The key to negotiation is to show that you have the upper hand]]
	- Use Urgency: Make them feel that time is limited
	- Use Bonuses: To add extra stuff on top 
	- Guarantee: Address all the objections 
	- Naming
		- MAGIC
			- Magnetic: reasons to buy
			- Avatar: Who is the customer? 
			- Goal: What do they want? 
			- Interval: Time-bound
			- C: Container word - Package, Blueprint
		- Example: 
			- 30-day dream job course
			- 90-day weight-loss challenge for nurses
